General approach

  • Five-card majors, 2/1 game forcing (see Two-over-one), 15–17 1NT.
  • Better minor with 3–3 and 4–4 in the minors; 1♦ with 4–5.
  • Opening style
    • First and second seat: rule of 20, with a sound suit or two quick tricks.
    • Third seat: any hand worth a lead-directing bid; responder keeps the auction low with Drury (see Drury).
    • Fourth seat: rule of 15 — high-card points plus spades.
  • Weak twos in ♦, ♥, and ♠: 6–10 high-card points, a six-card suit, no outside four-card major in first or second seat.
  • Strong, artificial 2♣ — see The strong 2♣ opening.

Notrump openings

The 1NT opening

  • 15–17 high-card points, balanced or semi-balanced.
    • Five-card majors allowed with 5-3-3-2 shape.
    • A six-card minor is fine with 6-3-2-2 shape and no weak doubleton.
    • Singleton honor with 5-4-3-1 is acceptable at the bottom of the range; treat 17 with a singleton as a suit opening.
  • A 2♣ response is Stayman; 2♦ and 2♥ are Jacoby transfers; 2♠ is a minor-suit relay (Minor-suit transfers).

Stayman

  • 1NT – 2♣ asks for a four-card major and promises nothing: responder may be weak with 4-4 in the majors and a short club, or 4-4-4-1 in any order except club length (garbage Stayman).
    • 2♦: no four-card major.
      • 2♥ or 2♠: five cards in the bid major and four in the other, invitational. Opener passes, raises, or corrects to the other major.
      • 3♥ or 3♠: Smolen — see Smolen.
      • 2NT: invitational, shows a four-card major (a direct 2NT would deny one).
      • 3♣ or 3♦: game forcing with a four-card major and five or more cards in the bid minor.
      • 3NT: to play, showing a four-card major that opener could not match.
      • Pass: garbage Stayman with diamond length.
    • 2♥: four hearts, possibly four spades as well.
      • 2♠: five spades and four hearts, invitational.
      • 2NT or 3NT: invitational or to play, denies four hearts, so implies four spades. Opener with four spades corrects to 3♠ or 4♠.
      • 3♥: invitational raise.
      • 3♠: slam try in hearts with a spade splinter; see Control bids for the continuations.
      • 4♥: to play.
    • 2♠: four spades, denies four hearts.
      • 2NT or 3NT: invitational or to play.
      • 3♠: invitational raise.
      • 3♥: game-forcing spade raise with slam interest; opener control bids or signs off.
        • 3♠: minimum, no slam interest. Responder may still continue with 4♣, 4♦, or 4♥ as control bids.
        • 4♣, 4♦, 4♥: control bid, accepting the try.
          • Responder continues with 4NT (see Roman key card 1430) or bids the cheapest control.
          • Responder signs off in 4♠ with nothing further to show.
        • 4♠: maximum but no side-suit control worth showing.
      • 4♠: to play.
  • After a 2♦ reply to Stayman, 3NT still lets opener correct to a five-card major, since opener may open 1NT with 5-3-3-2.

Smolen

  • After 1NT – 2♣ – 2♦, a jump to 3♥ or 3♠ shows 5-4 in the majors with game values, four cards in the bid suit and five in the other. Opener chooses between 3NT and the five-card major:
    • 3♥ (four hearts, five spades)
      • 3♠: three spades; responder continues naturally.
      • 3NT: two spades.
      • 4♠: three spades and a maximum with a source of tricks.
    • 3♠ (four spades, five hearts)
      • 4♦: transfer to hearts with three-card support, letting responder make a slam try below game.
        • 4♥: no slam interest.
        • 4♠, 5♣, 5♦: control bid with slam interest.
      • 3NT: two hearts.
  • With 5-5 in the majors, use a transfer to spades and then bid hearts; Smolen is never 5-5.

Jacoby transfers

  • 1NT – 2♦ shows five or more hearts; 1NT – 2♥ shows five or more spades. Opener completes the transfer with any hand, except:
    • Superaccept with four-card support and a maximum.
      • Jump to three of the major: four-card support, 17 points, no good doubleton to show.
      • Bid a new suit at the three level: four-card support and a doubleton in the bid suit. Responder retransfers with the cheaper bid.
      • 2NT: four-card support, 4-3-3-3 shape.
  • Responder’s continuations after the simple completion:
    • Pass: weak, five or six cards.
    • 2NT: invitational, exactly five cards. Opener passes, raises to 3NT, or bids three or four of the major with three-card support.
    • Three of the major: invitational, six cards.
    • 3NT: game values, five cards. Opener passes or corrects to four of the major.
    • A new suit at the three level: natural, game forcing, five or more cards.
      • Opener shows three-card support for the major first, then a stopper.
      • A jump in the fourth suit is a splinter.
    • Four of the major: to play, six cards, no slam interest. With slam interest, use Texas then 4NT.

Texas transfers

  • 1NT – 4♦ shows six or more hearts; 1NT – 4♥ shows six or more spades. Opener always completes.
  • Texas then 4NT is Roman key card for the major (Roman key card 1430); Jacoby then 4NT is quantitative.

Minor-suit transfers

  • 1NT – 2♠: relay to 3♣, either a weak signoff in clubs or diamonds, or a minor-suit slam try.
    • Opener bids 3♣ with a poor hand for a minor contract, 2NT with a good one (at least Q-x-x in both minors).
    • Responder passes 3♣, corrects to 3♦, or continues:
      • 3♥ or 3♠: shortness, both minors, game forcing.
      • 3NT: to play; the sequence denied a major.
  • 1NT – 2NT: transfer to diamonds, weak or strong. A direct invitational raise goes through Stayman instead.

Interference over 1NT

  • Over a natural or artificial overcall at the two level, responder uses lebensohl: a direct three-level bid is game forcing, and 2NT is a relay to 3♣ that either signs off in a suit below the overcall or continues with a slow, stopper-showing sequence.
    • Fast denies, slow shows: a direct 3NT denies a stopper in their suit, and 2NT then 3NT shows one.
    • Double is negative through 2♠ and penalty above that.
  • Over a double, redouble shows any 10 or more points; all bids are to play.
  • Over their 1NT overcall of our suit opening, see Versus their notrump.

The 2NT opening

  • 20–21 balanced. 3♣ is Stayman, 3♦ and 3♥ are transfers, 3♠ is a minor-suit relay, 4♦ and 4♥ are Texas. The same structures apply after a 2♣ opening and a 2NT rebid (The strong 2♣ opening).

Major-suit openings

Responses to one of a major

  • Single raise: 6–9 points, three-card support.
  • 1NT: forcing for one round (The forcing 1NT).
  • Two of a new suit: game forcing (Two-over-one).
  • 2NT: Jacoby (Jacoby 2NT).
  • Three of a minor, or three of the other major: Bergen (Bergen raises).
  • Double jump in a new suit: splinter (Splinters).
  • 1♥ – 1♠: four or more spades, 6 or more points, forcing.

The forcing 1NT

  • Forcing for one round by an unpassed hand; semi-forcing by a passed hand.
  • Opener’s rebids
    • Two of a lower suit: natural, may be a three-card minor.
    • Two of the same major: six cards, or a good five with no convenient rebid.
    • Jump shift: game forcing, 18 or more.
    • 2NT: 18–19 balanced.
  • Responder’s continuations
    • A new suit at the two level: to play, five or more cards.
    • Two of opener’s major: a three-card limit raise or a doubleton with a maximum.
    • 2NT: 11–12, invitational.
    • Three of opener’s major: a three-card limit raise with a good hand.
    • Jump to three of a new suit: invitational, a good six-card suit.

Two-over-one

  • A two-level response in a new suit by an unpassed hand is game forcing.
    • 1♠ – 2♥ promises five hearts. 1♥ – 2♦ and 1♥ – 2♣ may be four cards.
    • Responder’s rebid of the same suit shows six.
  • Opener’s rebids are natural; a jump shows extras and a good suit, and a reverse shows shape without extra values.
  • Support for responder’s suit at the three level is a genuine raise; 2NT shows a balanced minimum with stoppers.
  • Slow arrival: bidding game directly shows a minimum; taking a slower route shows slam interest (Control bids).

Jacoby 2NT

  • Four-card support, game forcing. Opener describes shape and strength:
    • A new suit at the three level: singleton or void.
    • Three of the major: extras, no shortness.
    • 3NT: a balanced minimum.
    • A new suit at the four level: a good five-card side suit.
    • Four of the major: minimum, no shortness.
  • Responder continues with control bids or 4NT (Roman key card 1430).

Bergen raises

  • 3♣: four-card support, 7–9 points. 3♦: four-card support, 10–12.
  • Three of the other major: a mixed raise, four-card support, 7–9 with a good suit.
  • Three of opener’s major: preemptive, four-card support, 0–6.
  • Over interference, Bergen is off; raises are natural.

Splinters

  • A double jump in a new suit shows four-card support, game values, and a singleton or void in the bid suit.
  • Opener signs off with wasted values in the short suit, or control bids with slam interest.

Drury

  • By a passed hand, 2♣ over a major opening shows three or more trumps with a limit raise. Opener rebids two of the major with a light opening; any other bid is natural and shows a full opener.

Minor-suit openings

Inverted minors

  • A single raise of a minor is 10 or more points, forcing to 2NT or three of the minor, and denies a four-card major.
  • A jump raise is preemptive, five-card support, 0–6.
  • After 1♣ – 2♣ or 1♦ – 2♦, opener bids a stopper, 2NT with a minimum, or three of the minor with a minimum and an unbalanced hand.

Walsh responses

  • Over 1♣, bypass a four-card or five-card diamond suit with a four-card major and less than game values. Bidding 1♦ and then a major shows five diamonds and game-going values.

The strong 2♣ opening

  • 2♣: 22 or more balanced, or any hand with nine or more playing tricks.
  • 2♦ is waiting; 2♥ is a double negative, showing a very bad hand.
  • 2♣ – 2♦ – 2NT: 22–24, with the 2NT structure (The 2NT opening).

Competitive bidding

Overcalls

  • Simple overcalls: 8–16, a five-card suit at the one level; a good suit at the two level.
  • Jump overcalls are weak, six cards.
  • 1NT overcall: 15–18 with a stopper; systems on.
  • Responses to an overcall
    • A raise is competitive; a cue bid is a limit raise or better.
    • A new suit is constructive, not forcing.
    • A jump in a new suit is fit-showing, showing the suit and support.

Takeout doubles

  • Shape suitable or 17 or more of any shape.
  • Responses
    • A jump is invitational, 9–11.
    • A cue bid is game forcing.
    • 1NT: 6–10 with a stopper.

Negative doubles

  • Through 3♠.
  • Over 1♣ or 1♦ – 1♥, the double shows exactly four spades; 1♠ shows five.
  • Over an overcall of 1♠, the double shows four hearts, five or more when the double is at the two level.

Support doubles

  • When responder bids a major at the one level and the next hand bids or doubles, opener’s double or redouble shows exactly three-card support. A direct raise promises four.

Versus their notrump

  • Over their 1NT opening, strong or weak: Woolsey — see Woolsey.
  • Over their 1NT overcall of our opening, double is penalty; all suit bids are to play, and a cue bid is a game-forcing raise.

Slam bidding

Roman key card 1430

  • 4NT asks for key cards; the responses are 1 or 4, 3 or 0, 2 without the trump queen, 2 with the trump queen.
    • After a 5♣ or 5♦ reply, the cheapest non-trump bid asks for the queen.
      • The cheapest bid denies the queen.
      • Bidding a suit shows the queen and a king in the bid suit.
      • Bidding six of the trump suit shows the queen without an outside king.
    • 5NT asks for specific kings; responder bids the cheapest king, or six of the trump suit with none.
  • When hearts are trumps, the queen ask over 5♦ is 5♠; when spades are trumps, it is the cheapest suit below five of the trumps.
  • Trumps are the last suit bid naturally and raised, or the last suit bid by the 4NT bidder if none was raised.

Exclusion

  • A jump to the five level in a new suit after a suit is agreed asks for key cards outside the bid suit. Responses follow 1430 starting at the next step.

Control bids

  • Italian style: first- or second-round controls, bid up the line, so a bypassed suit denies a control in it.
  • The first control bid below game asks partner to cooperate with the cheapest control; returning to the trump suit denies one.

Defensive carding

Opening leads

  1. Against suits: fourth best from length with an honor, top of a doubleton, low from three small, and the top of a sequence.
    • Ace from A-K, except at the five level or higher where the king asks for count.
    • The king from K-Q asks for attitude.
  2. Against notrump: fourth best; the ace asks for an unblock or count; the queen from K-Q-10-9 asks for an unblock of the jack.

Signals

  • Standard attitude on partner’s lead; upside-down count when count is requested.
  • Suit preference when the situation is clearly not attitude or count: a high card asks for the higher-ranking suit, a low card for the lower.
  • Trump echo shows the ability to ruff.
